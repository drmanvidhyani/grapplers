Quick guide

When should you get it checked?

Not sure if it's serious? Use this first.

Emergency: call 112 now

  • Neck injury with numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs
  • Head knock with confusion, vomiting, or blacking out
  • Not coming round normally after being choked out
  • Chest pain or difficulty breathing after a crush or knee-on-belly
  • An obviously deformed joint or a bone out of place

See a physio within a few days

  • You felt or heard a pop, then swelling within hours
  • A joint gives way, locks or catches
  • You can't fully straighten or bend the joint
  • A shoulder that felt like it "came out", even if it went back in
  • A fingertip that droops and won't straighten on its own

Book an assessment if it's lingering

  • Pain still there after 1–2 weeks of normal training
  • It keeps coming back in the same spot
  • You're avoiding positions, grips or sides because of it
  • You're relying on painkillers or tape just to train
Know the signs

Common grappling injuries

Tap each one to see how it happens and when to consult us.

01

Knee

Leg locks, guard passing, takedowns

›

How it happens

Twisting under load in leg-lock exchanges, knee reaps, guard passing pressure and scrambles. Ligaments (MCL, ACL) and the meniscus take the strain, and damage from heel hooks can occur before you feel pain.

Consult us if

  • You felt a pop and the knee swelled within hours
  • It gives way when you pivot, scramble or stand up
  • It locks, catches, or won't straighten fully
  • Pain on the inside or outside of the knee when you twist
Don't keep rolling on it. An unstable knee that keeps giving way can damage the cartilage further.
02

Shoulder

Kimuras, americanas, omoplatas, falls

›

How it happens

Rotational shoulder locks, posting a hand on a takedown, and landing on the point of the shoulder. Common results are rotator cuff strains, AC joint sprains and instability after a partial or full dislocation.

Consult us if

  • It felt like it slipped out, even if it went back in
  • Pain at night or when lying on that side
  • Weakness or pain lifting the arm overhead or behind your back
  • A bump or tenderness on top of the shoulder after a fall
A shoulder that has slipped once is more likely to slip again. Get it assessed before you go back to full rolling.
03

Elbow

Armbars, posting, late taps

›

How it happens

Over-straightening in armbars, especially on a late tap, and posting on a straight arm in a scramble. This stretches the ligaments on the inside of the elbow.

Consult us if

  • Pain on the inside of the elbow, especially when gripping
  • You can't fully straighten or bend the arm
  • Swelling or bruising after a submission
  • The elbow feels loose or unstable
Tap early. Elbow ligaments heal slowly, and a second hyperextension usually sets you back further than the first.
04

Fingers, hand & wrist

Gi grips, grip fighting, posting

›

How it happens

Fingers caught in sleeves and lapels, grips stripped at speed, and posting on the wrist. Sprained finger joints, strained tendons and wrist sprains are common, and often ignored.

Consult us if

  • A finger joint stays swollen or stiff for more than 2 weeks
  • You can't fully bend or straighten a finger
  • A fingertip droops and won't straighten on its own: get it seen within days
  • Wrist pain on the thumb side after a fall onto your hand
Finger injuries left untreated can stiffen permanently. Early splinting and the right exercises make a real difference.
05

Neck

Cranks, stacking, guillotines

›

How it happens

Neck cranks, can-openers, being stacked in guard, and defending guillotines. Most cases are muscle and joint strains, but the neck needs care.

Consult us if

  • Stiffness or pain turning your head that lasts more than a few days
  • Headaches that started after training
  • Pain that spreads to the shoulder blade or arm
Numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs after a neck injury is an emergency. Stop, stay still and call 112.
06

Lower back

Stacking, guard retention, wrestling

›

How it happens

Repeated flexion and twisting in guard, being stacked, and lifting opponents in wrestling exchanges.

Consult us if

  • Back pain that hasn't improved after a week
  • Pain spreading into the buttock or down the leg
  • Pain that's worse every time you play guard
Numbness in the groin area or trouble controlling your bladder or bowels is an emergency. Call 112.
07

Ribs

Knee-on-belly, pressure passing

›

How it happens

Heavy top pressure, knee-on-belly and body-lock squeezes. Bruised or strained rib cartilage is common; cracked ribs happen too.

Consult us if

  • Sharp pain when you breathe deeply, cough or laugh
  • Pain lying on that side, or twisting
  • It isn't easing after 1–2 weeks
Shortness of breath or chest pain after a rib injury is an emergency. Call 112.
08

Ankle & foot

Straight ankle locks, takedowns

›

How it happens

Ankle locks, rolling the ankle in scrambles and takedowns, and toes caught on the mat.

Consult us if

  • You can't put weight on it, or it's very swollen
  • It keeps rolling or feels weak weeks after a sprain
  • You need to tape it just to train
Repeat ankle sprains do more damage each time. Rehab now saves you a chronic problem later.

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis. Only an assessment can tell you what's going on.
